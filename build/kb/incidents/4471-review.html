The known incident says one issue and one queued fix. That claim is too broad. This review separates the shared parser, the booking form hint, and reports whose symptoms need an independent investigation. The export is a snapshot from 4 October; this review does not establish the live queue’s current state.

File by behavior, not by label

The shared parser reads month first although the tenant writes day first. Reproduce with a day-first tenant through the app or email path: 07/04 becomes 4 July rather than 7 April. Fix parsing at the boundary using the declared tenant format, validate input, and test ambiguous and unambiguous dates.

Booking-v3 displays MM/DD/YYYY but reads input using the tenant’s day-first convention. This affects form reports from Alder Physio, Copper Studio, and Pale Harbour. Reproduce by following the hint and entering 04/07 for 7 April: a day-first reader produces 4 July. Make the hint, validation, and actual form interpretation agree. Fixing the shared parser alone does not fix this misleading form.

Some reports describe adjacent-day shifts, differences between recipients, or an invite arriving before booking. Keep these in a separate investigation rather than declaring the parser responsible. Compare raw booking dates, timestamps, event IDs, timezone conversions, delivery logs, and client rendering. Timezone handling is a hypothesis; the export does not establish a root cause, and the chronology report might be separate again.

App and email reports can share one parser fault despite different subjects. The same subject can refer to parser or form-hint faults. Even two form reports can differ because their tenants run different form versions. Unknown tenants are not safe to merge merely because subjects match. Identify tenant, locale, form version, channel, expected behavior, and observed behavior before merging; keep uncertain cases apart for investigation.
